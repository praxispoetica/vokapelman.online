## Reading the Ledger

By midnight, the ledger lay open under the amber circle of lamplight. Its pages were filled with their father's cramped, deliberate script: bushels of apples sold, pounds of lard, days hired out to neighboring farms, and notes on debts that had ceased to matter thirty years ago.

"The lower pasture goes to the town bank in November if the title isn't cleared," Clara said, leaning across the pine tabletop. Her pencil moved quickly over a scrap of paper, calculating numbers that never seemed to balance.

Arthur stood by the window, watching the moonlight pool across the dead grass. "Then let it go. We never grew anything worth keeping down there anyway."

"He broke his wrist clearing those stones, Arthur."

"And spent the rest of his life complaining about the damp," he murmured. "Sentiment won't pay the interest."

## The Morning Mist

Dawn rose without warmth, silver and thick as river water across the orchard slope. In the bare pear trees, a lone robin rustled among the dead leaves, its orange breast a sharp wound of color in the gray dawn.

Clara came down the porch steps with her coat buttoned to the chin, her boots leaving dark tracks in the rime. She held two cups of chicory coffee, steaming into the chill air.

"The lawyer from the county arrives at ten," she said, offering him a mug.

Arthur took it, feeling the warmth seep through his calloused fingers. For a long moment, neither spoke, listening to the small bird call twice from the hedge and then take wing toward the timberline.

"We tell him we keep the house," Arthur said quietly. "Whatever else they take, the roof stays ours."
