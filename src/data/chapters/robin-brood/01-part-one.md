## Arrival at the Gate

The orchard had surrendered its fruit weeks ago, leaving only the gnarled black branches against a low, bruised sky. When the gate creaked open, Arthur did not look up from the split rail fence. He knew his sister’s step as well as his own breath, though twelve years had passed since she had walked this lane.

"You haven't painted it," Clara said, stopping three paces shy.

"Paint costs money," Arthur said, resting his hammer. "And the wood remembers how to rot without help."

She carried one canvas bag, scuffed at the corners, and wore a woolen coat that had seen better winters in the city. Behind her, the valley was turning purple with evening frost.

## The Cold Hearth

Inside the kitchen, the iron stove was cold to the touch. The table where four of them had once eaten porridge before sunrise now held only a stack of yellowing newspapers, an oil lamp with a cracked chimney, and their father's pocket ledger bound in pigskin.

"He kept every receipt," Arthur said, striking a match against the iron box. The sulfur flared, casting their shadows long against the whitewashed wall. "Even the feed bills from the drought summer."

Clara ran two fingers over the leather cover without opening it. "Did he ask for me?"

Arthur blew out the match and let the smoke drift between them before answering. "He asked what time the train was due. That was all."
